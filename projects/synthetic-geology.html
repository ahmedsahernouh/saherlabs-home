Synthetic modeling · November 2026

Geology and geometry, made visible.

A braided-river landscape, an imposed graben and mathematical patterns explore how surfaces, polygons, fault objects and synthetic wells can be constructed and explained.

Synthetic modeling gallery

River and grabenGeometric experiments

Explore two synthetic river views

The channel pattern visible in Landsat imagery provides a starting shape. Terrain elevations, scour, gravel-bar relief and fault displacement are modeled illustrations in a 10 × 10 km local frame, rather than measured topography or an interpretation of the source landscape.

The river view compares water coverage with exposed model scour. The graben view introduces two modeled normal faults and an imposed 26 m downthrow. The previews use different vertical exaggeration—approximately 69× and 27×—so their apparent relief should not be compared at the same scale.

Channel imagery reference: NASA Earth Observatory / Michala Garrison, using USGS Landsat data. View the source image and story.

Mathematical patterns as geometry experiments

These generated patterns exercise polygon boundaries, fault sticks and synthetic well placement in a local coordinate frame. Gold crosshair rings mark synthetic wellheads; gold lines in the oblique views show synthetic boreholes.

The images were rendered from generated geometry outside Petrel, with 1.65× depth exaggeration in the oblique views.

From patterns to spatial objects

These experiments bring together terrain surfaces, channel geometry, polygon boundaries, fault sticks and synthetic wells. The river models explore the relationship between landforms and imposed fault displacement; the mathematical patterns explore spatial organization at different scales.

All gallery views are synthetic illustrations rendered outside Petrel.
